## Full list

!! text

**William Herschel:** some striking atmospheric moments, some of the linking passages feel half arsed

**John Marsh:** some surprising banger tunes in here! Conversation Symphony is a cool idea (split the orchestra into two)

**Josef Mysliveček:** this sure is some music. Not sure I have anything else to say about it.

**Carl Stamitz:** I already like Stamitz (viola concerto). Feels polished but I'm not really getting excited by anything here. Edit: went back to this and loved it, so not sure what went wrong the first time round. The last movement of the hunt symphony was my favourite. Good enough that I dropped this project for a few weeks and went on a general Stamitz listening binge.

**Samuel Wesley:** wow I like this a lot. Vaguely similar to Marsh but bigger somehow, more ambitious and dramatic. Not quite the same talent for banger tunes but overall it feels better constructed. Maybe I actually just like English classical music more than I realised. Apparently it kept the Handel influence longer than elsewhere, which can mean "dull and pompous" but when it works it works.

**Franz Anton Hoffmeister:** Also has a viola concerto I like. These didn't grab me at all for whatever reason.

**Václav Pichl:** The slow movements are really nice. Particularly the one in the "Diana" symphony. Very listenable in general.

**Christian Cannabich:** feels like an endless stream of lightweight opera overture type music. Fine as background music but not excited about it

**Johann Baptist Vanhal:** nice enough to listen to but nothing particularly excited me

**Leopold Koželuch:** this is great! Stylish and has actual tunes. I'd been wading through a lot of "this is ok background music I guess" so this was very refreshing

**Antonio Salieri:** this one is a bit different, with more opera overtures and incidental music. There's one piece where I thought "wow, this is _really_ overstaying its welcome", then I checked and found that a) it was called "Twenty-six variations on 'La follia di Spagna'" and b) I wasn't even halfway through yet. I survived the full 18 minutes but don't feel the need to repeat it

**Georg Joseph Vogler:** lots of ballet type music which seems fine if you're into that sort of thing, but I'm not particularly

**Adalbert Gyrowetz:** unobjectionable, blocked out noise perfectly well on a busy office day

**Carles Baguer:** I found this extremely boring

**Ignace Pleyel:** This feels much more substantial and interesting. There's something slightly different going on stylistically here that I can't quite put my finger on. I appreciate the quality but don't feel that strongly into it.

**Antonio Rosetti:** This is fine but as with Pleyel I'm not getting excited by it.

---

_At this point I was starting to feel a bit burned out on mid tier 18th-century music and had a break for a bit. Back to it a month or so later..._

---

**Luigi Boccherini:** I love Streets of Madrid but haven't got much out of any other Boccherini. This is pretty listenable but there's nothing that really jumps out as interesting.

**Michael Haydn:** I loved this! Listened on repeat for weeks, biggest hit after Stamitz. He's Joseph's little brother, which checks out - to me it feels like there's some shared sunny expansive quality.

**François-Joseph Gossec:** Enjoyable enough but kind of samey - constant stream of pleasant background music with no memorable bits. The one exception is the second symphony in E flat major, which has some recording error in the first movement that inserted an unpleasant noise that gave me a jump scare, so I didn't repeat-listen to that one.

**Franz Krommer:** bit later, sounds more like Beethoven than Mozart. Nothing wrong with that but not particularly what I was looking for. Listened to it once and moved on.

**Muzio Clementi:** feels a bit later too. Well done but in a sort of light music that doesn't excite me that much

**Paul Wranitsky:** this is also good but in a later style that isn't really the sound I want. There's a selection effect where a lot of these remaining recordings are ones I tried at some point, thought "hm maybe not" and put back in the jar. Now I can't avoid eating them.

**Franz Xaver Richter:** ok this last one is more in the style I was looking for, liked the trumpet symphony
